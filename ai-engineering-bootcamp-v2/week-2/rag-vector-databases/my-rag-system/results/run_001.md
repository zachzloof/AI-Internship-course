## Questions & Answers

**Q: What is Bitcoin?**

A: Bitcoin is a decentralized digital currency that enables individuals to send and receive value directly, without the need for banks, payment processors, or any central authority. It was introduced in 2008 by an individual or group using the pseudonym Satoshi Nakamoto, who published a white paper titled "Bitcoin: A Peer-to-Peer Electronic Cash System." The Bitcoin network became operational in January 2009 with the mining of the "genesis block," which is the first block in the Bitcoin blockchain.

Bitcoin is significant because it provided a solution to a long-standing challenge in computer science: creating scarce, transferable digital money without relying on a trusted central party. Its core innovations—combining cryptography, a distributed ledger, and economic incentives—have laid the groundwork for the broader cryptocurrency and blockchain ecosystem that has developed since its inception.

**Q: What is the blockchain?**

A: The blockchain is a public, distributed ledger that records every transaction made on the Bitcoin network. Unlike traditional banking systems where a central authority maintains records, the blockchain relies on thousands of computers, known as nodes, around the world, each keeping their own copy of the entire transaction history. Transactions are grouped into "blocks," and each block is cryptographically linked to the previous one, forming a continuous chain.

This technology addresses a significant challenge in creating scarce, transferable digital money without the need for a trusted central party. Bitcoin's innovation in combining cryptography, a distributed ledger, and economic incentives has laid the groundwork for the broader cryptocurrency and blockchain ecosystem that has developed since its inception.

**Q: How does Bitcoin mining work?**

A: Bitcoin mining is the process through which new transactions are added to the blockchain. It involves participants known as miners, who use specialized computer hardware to solve complex mathematical puzzles. This system is based on a mechanism called Proof-of-Work, which requires significant computational effort to find a valid solution, although verifying that solution is straightforward for other computers.

The first miner to successfully solve the puzzle gets the opportunity to add the next block to the blockchain and is rewarded with newly created bitcoins, along with any transaction fees included in that block. This reward is referred to as the "block subsidy." Additionally, the difficulty of mining adjusts approximately every two weeks to ensure that, on average, a new block is added to the blockchain every ten minutes, regardless of the total computing power available on the network.

**Q: What is the maximum supply of Bitcoin?**

A: The maximum supply of Bitcoin is capped at 21 million coins. This limit is enforced by the Bitcoin software, and it is designed to ensure that no more than this amount will ever exist. The process of "halving," which occurs roughly every four years, reduces the block reward for miners, thereby slowing the rate at which new bitcoins are introduced into circulation. It is expected that the last fractional bitcoin will be mined around the year 2140.

**Q: What is the difference between a public key and a private key in a Bitcoin wallet?**

A: In a Bitcoin wallet, the difference between a public key and a private key is fundamental to the security and functionality of the wallet:

1. **Public Key**: This is derived from the private key and is used to create a public wallet address. It can be shared with others to receive Bitcoin. The public key allows others to verify that a transaction was signed by the corresponding private key without revealing the private key itself.

2. **Private Key**: This is a secret piece of data that must be kept confidential. It is used to digitally sign transactions, authorizing the transfer of Bitcoin from the wallet. Whoever controls the private key controls the funds associated with that wallet. This is why the phrase "not your keys, not your coins" is prevalent in the Bitcoin community; if someone else has access to your private key, they can access your funds.

In summary, the public key is for receiving funds and can be shared, while the private key is for signing transactions and must be kept secret to maintain control over the Bitcoin in the wallet.
