## Questions & Answers

**Q: What is Bitcoin?**

A: Bitcoin is a decentralized digital currency that allows people to send and receive value directly, without relying on banks, payment processors, or any central authority. It was introduced in 2008 by a person or group using the pseudonym Satoshi Nakamoto and went live in January 2009. Bitcoin operates on a global network of computers that follow the same open-source rules and is not controlled by any single entity. 

At its core, Bitcoin uses a blockchain, which is a public, distributed ledger that records every transaction made on the network. This ledger is maintained by thousands of computers worldwide, ensuring the integrity and security of the transaction history. 

(Source: Understanding Bitcoin)

**Q: What is the blockchain?**

A: The blockchain is a public, distributed ledger that records every transaction ever made on the Bitcoin network. It consists of blocks that are cryptographically linked to one another, forming a chain. Each block references the unique hash of the previous block, making it extremely difficult to alter any past transaction without redoing the work for every subsequent block on a majority of the network's computing power simultaneously. This structure ensures the integrity and security of the transaction history. 

(Source: Understanding Bitcoin)

**Q: How does Bitcoin mining work?**

A: Bitcoin mining is the process through which new transactions are added to the blockchain. Miners use specialized computer hardware to compete in solving a difficult mathematical puzzle, which is part of a system called Proof-of-Work. This requires significant computational effort to find a valid solution, but verifying that solution is easy for other computers. The first miner to solve the puzzle gets to add the next block to the blockchain and is rewarded with newly created bitcoin and any transaction fees included in that block, known as the "block subsidy." Mining difficulty adjusts approximately every two weeks to ensure that a new block is found roughly every ten minutes, regardless of the total computing power on the network. 

(Source: Understanding Bitcoin, Mining and Proof-of-Work)

**Q: What is the maximum supply of Bitcoin?**

A: The maximum supply of Bitcoin is 21 million. This cap is enforced by the software that every node runs. [Source: Understanding Bitcoin, Limited Supply and Halving]

**Q: What is the difference between a public key and a private key in a Bitcoin wallet?**

A: A public key is derived from a private key and is used to create a public wallet address. It can be shared with others to receive Bitcoin. A private key, on the other hand, is a secret piece of data that must be kept confidential, as it allows the owner to control and authorize transactions from the wallet. The phrase "not your keys, not your coins" emphasizes the importance of keeping the private key secure. 

(Source: Understanding Bitcoin, Wallets and Keys)
