## Questions & Answers

**Q: What is Bitcoin?**

A: Bitcoin is a decentralized digital currency that enables people to send and receive value directly, without the need for banks, payment processors, or any central authority. It was introduced in 2008 by an individual or group using the pseudonym Satoshi Nakamoto, who published a white paper titled "Bitcoin: A Peer-to-Peer Electronic Cash System." The Bitcoin network went live in January 2009 with the mining of the "genesis block," the first block in the Bitcoin blockchain. 

Bitcoin addresses a significant challenge in creating scarce, transferable digital money without a trusted central party, and it combines cryptography, a distributed ledger, and economic incentives, which has laid the groundwork for the broader cryptocurrency and blockchain ecosystem that followed.

**Q: What is the blockchain?**

A: The blockchain is a public, distributed ledger that records every transaction made on the Bitcoin network. Instead of a single entity like a bank maintaining a private record, thousands of computers, known as nodes, each keep their own copy of the entire transaction history. Transactions are grouped into "blocks," and each block is cryptographically linked to the previous one, forming a chain (source).

**Q: How does Bitcoin mining work?**

A: Bitcoin mining involves adding new transactions to the blockchain through a process where miners use specialized computer hardware to solve complex mathematical puzzles. This system is known as Proof-of-Work, which requires significant computational effort to find a valid solution, although verifying that solution is easy for other computers.

The first miner to solve the puzzle gets to add the next block to the blockchain and receives a reward, which includes newly created bitcoin and any transaction fees from that block. This reward is referred to as the "block subsidy." The mining difficulty adjusts approximately every two weeks to ensure that a new block is found roughly every ten minutes, regardless of the total computing power on the network.

**Q: What is the maximum supply of Bitcoin?**

A: The maximum supply of Bitcoin is 21 million. This limit is enforced by the software that every node runs, and it is designed to ensure that no more than this amount will ever exist. The process of "halving" occurs roughly every four years, reducing the block reward for miners and slowing the rate at which new bitcoins are created. By around the year 2140, the last fractional bitcoin is expected to be mined (source).

**Q: What is the difference between a public key and a private key in a Bitcoin wallet?**

A: In a Bitcoin wallet, the public key is used to derive a public wallet address, while the private key is a secret piece of data that controls the funds. The private key is used to digitally sign transactions, proving ownership without revealing the key itself. Essentially, whoever controls the private key controls the associated funds, which is why the phrase "not your keys, not your coins" is emphasized in the Bitcoin community.
