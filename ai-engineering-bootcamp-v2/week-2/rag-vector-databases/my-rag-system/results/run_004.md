## Questions & Answers

**Q: What is Bitcoin?**

A: Bitcoin is a decentralized digital currency that enables direct value transfer between individuals without the need for banks or central authorities. It was introduced in 2008 by an entity using the pseudonym Satoshi Nakamoto and became operational in January 2009 with the mining of the first block, known as the "genesis block." Bitcoin operates on a global network of computers following open-source rules, and it is not controlled by any single entity. Its core innovation combines cryptography, a distributed ledger (the blockchain), and economic incentives, which has laid the groundwork for the broader cryptocurrency ecosystem. The blockchain serves as a public ledger that records all transactions, making it difficult to alter past records due to its cryptographic structure (source: Understanding Bitcoin).

**Q: What is the blockchain?**

A: The blockchain is a public, distributed ledger that records every transaction made on the Bitcoin network. It consists of thousands of computers, known as nodes, each maintaining their own copy of the entire transaction history. Transactions are grouped into "blocks," which are cryptographically linked to one another, forming a chain. This structure makes it extremely difficult to alter any past transaction, as doing so would require redoing the work for every subsequent block across a majority of the network's computing power simultaneously (source: Understanding Bitcoin).

**Q: How does Bitcoin mining work?**

A: Bitcoin mining is the process through which new transactions are added to the blockchain. Miners use specialized computer hardware to compete in solving a difficult mathematical puzzle, a system known as Proof-of-Work. This requires significant computational effort to find a valid solution, which is easy for other computers to verify. The first miner to solve the puzzle gets to add the next block to the blockchain and is rewarded with newly created bitcoin and any transaction fees from that block, known as the "block subsidy." The mining difficulty adjusts approximately every two weeks to ensure that a new block is found roughly every ten minutes, regardless of the total computing power on the network. 

(Source: Understanding Bitcoin, Mining and Proof-of-Work)

**Q: What is the maximum supply of Bitcoin?**

A: The maximum supply of Bitcoin is capped at 21 million coins. This limit is enforced by the software that every node runs, and it is designed to slow the rate at which new bitcoins enter circulation through events known as "halvings" that occur roughly every four years (source: Understanding Bitcoin).

**Q: What is the difference between a public key and a private key in a Bitcoin wallet?**

A: In a Bitcoin wallet, the public key is used to derive a public wallet address, which can be shared with others to receive funds. The private key, on the other hand, is a secret piece of data that allows the owner to digitally sign transactions, thereby authorizing the transfer of Bitcoin. Control of the private key means control over the funds, which is why it is crucial to keep it secure. The phrase "not your keys, not your coins" emphasizes the importance of safeguarding the private key (source: Understanding Bitcoin).
